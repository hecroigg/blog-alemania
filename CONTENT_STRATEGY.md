# Living Germany content strategy

## Editorial north star

Publish the clearest next step for a person building a life in Germany. Utility, trust, SEO, speed, design, and monetisation are the priority order. A page should exist because it resolves an intent—not because a keyword can be generated.

## Content architecture

1. **Moving to Germany** — eligibility context, planning, arrival, documents, first 30 days.
2. **Bureaucracy** — Anmeldung, identifiers, permits, recurring public obligations, leaving Germany.
3. **Work** — job discovery, applications, contracts, payroll, workplace rights, employment types, self-employment.
4. **Housing** — discovery, applications, rental language, contracts, deposits, utilities, tenant basics.
5. **Money** — banking, everyday payment, taxes, cost planning, credit records, consumer protection.
6. **Healthcare & insurance** — system orientation, coverage routes, accessing care, common policies.
7. **Transport** — local associations, rail, tickets, driving, cycling, mobility decisions.
8. **Daily life** — connectivity, shopping, Pfand, recycling, opening hours, language, etiquette.
9. **Cities** — unique local decision guides with municipal sources and region-aware context.
10. **Audience hubs** — curated journeys for students, workers, families, freelancers, EU citizens, and non-EU citizens.

The guide is the atomic editorial unit. Category, city, and audience pages organise guides without duplicating their answers.

## Cluster and internal-linking rules

- Every guide links up to one category hub.
- Every guide links to two to four next-step guides based on task dependency, not keyword similarity.
- City pages link to housing, Anmeldung, cost of living, transport, and relevant national guides.
- Audience hubs curate a task order and must not restate complete guides.
- When a term needs a full explanation (for example SCHUFA), define it briefly and link to the canonical guide.
- Use descriptive anchors. Avoid repeated “click here” or artificial exact-match anchors.
- Source links should point to the organisation that owns the process whenever possible.

## Programmatic SEO opportunities

### Cities

Use `/cities/[city]` only when the record contains a unique introduction, locally relevant housing and transport context, current municipal links, and at least one useful local distinction. Later structured fields can include:

- municipality and federal state;
- official registration and immigration-office URLs;
- local transport association;
- universities and research institutions;
- major employment sectors (sourced, not scraped claims);
- dated rent or housing indicators with methodology;
- region-aware commuting notes;
- review date and source owner.

Do not publish an automatically generated page when the only unique value is the city name.

### Origin-to-Germany pages

Pages such as “moving from Spain” or “moving from the USA” should exist only after nationality-specific rights, document preparation, tax/residency interactions, and official consular sources are researched. They must not be a generic moving guide with a country token.

### Decision tables

Structured, maintainable comparison data can support pages for insurance types, transport products, banking features, and housing terms. Each field needs a source, last-checked date, and update owner. Commercial relationships must not control ranking logic.

## Content quality gate

Before publishing, confirm:

- the page resolves one clear intent;
- the direct answer appears near the top;
- variable facts have a date and authoritative source;
- legal, tax, medical, and financial boundaries are explicit;
- examples do not become universal claims;
- headings describe reader questions rather than keyword variants;
- FAQs add information rather than repeat headings;
- every internal link helps the next task;
- every external link is live and belongs to the relevant source owner;
- the page has an accountable review date.

## Measurement plan

At launch, prioritise non-invasive, consent-aware measurement:

- organic landing pages and qualified scroll depth;
- guide-to-guide continuation rate;
- search terms returning no results;
- newsletter intent once a provider is connected;
- source-link use on time-sensitive pages;
- corrections received and time to resolution;
- Core Web Vitals by template.

Do not optimise success around pageviews alone. Returning readers, successful journeys, and low correction rates are stronger trust signals.
